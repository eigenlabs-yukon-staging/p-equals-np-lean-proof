# Exact SAT with a polynomial product of wide-clause widths

This submission proves an exact polynomial-time language theorem for canonical CNF formulae whose wide-clause choice count fits a fixed polynomial budget. A wide clause is one with more than two literals. The budget counts the product of their widths, using one choice when the wide part is empty. It allows an input-dependent number of wide clauses and arbitrary input-dependent widths when the combined choice space is small enough. The construction uses mixed-radix branch indices and the pinned deterministic 2-SAT machine, with an explicit measured guard for the product bound.

## Mixed-radix arithmetic and unit generation

`MeasuredUnaryDivMod` proves a measured quotient and remainder function for a tagged pair of Boolean words. Their lengths are the numerator and divisor. The machine maintains the unchanged divisor, the remaining numerator and a unary quotient. If the divisor is nonzero and fits into the remaining numerator, it drops that many symbols and emits one quotient bit. Otherwise it leaves the state unchanged. At most the original numerator length many rounds suffice. The arbitrary-state proof bounds growth by a fixed number of bits including pair delimiters. The output normalizes the remainder into an all-true word, and the exact statement includes the convention that dividing by zero gives quotient zero and the original length as remainder.

`MeasuredClauseLiteralCount` scans aligned reference code pairs and emits a bit whenever a literal terminator is encountered. On the canonical encoding of a clause, the result has exactly one bit per literal. This gives the radix for the head clause without decoding an unbounded binary variable index as a unary number. The candidate branch machine also uses the earlier first-clause splitter and unary literal selector with their exact output semantics.

`MeasuredMixedRadixUnits` scans a canonical extra formula from the front. For each clause of width m it divides the current unary index by m. The remainder selects one literal from that clause. The quotient is passed to the next clause. The selected literal is emitted as a unit clause. An absent literal corresponds to the encoded empty clause, which no assignment satisfies. The state carries the remaining encoded formula, the remaining index, and the accumulated unit encoding. Replacing a clause by its selected unit uses at most the clause's space. For arbitrary raw states the machine clips the emitted word to the separated clause length. On canonical states the proof shows that clipping is inactive. The divider never lengthens its quotient. These bounds give a constant-growth step and a measured loop with the extra formula's encoded length as clock.

The semantic theorem has two directions. Any satisfying assignment for the generated unit formula satisfies the original extra formula, since the generated unit at each position asserts a literal from that clause. Conversely, a satisfying assignment for the extra formula supplies an index below the product of its clause widths. The proof recursively chooses positions of true literals and constructs the index as the current position plus the current width times the remainder of the index construction. That expression has the desired quotient and remainder and stays below the product. All of these facts are proved over the exact reference clause and assignment definitions.

## Branching and exact guarding

`ProductBudgetSAT` precomposes this branch generator with the previously measured wide-clause partition on ordinary canonical CNF encodings. It keeps the width-at-most-two part unchanged, uses the mixed-radix generator on the wide part, and appends the resulting unit formula. All generated formulae have width at most two. The indexed projection uses the original input length's natural-polynomial budget as clock and the pinned 2-SAT machine as verifier. When the width product fits that budget, the satisfying-assignment index proved by the mixed-radix completeness argument is among the generated candidates. Satisfiability is invariant under the reversal of the two partitioned lists.

`MeasuredCappedClauseProduct` measures the branch-space size. Its arithmetic output is the smaller of a supplied unary cap and the actual product of clause widths. It accumulates a clipped product, starting with a clipped unary one, and processes one clause per round. A natural-number lemma shows that clipping before each multiplication gives the correct clipped final value. It handles a zero radix as well, including after a previous saturated multiplication. The retained accumulator length is bounded by the cap and the cap never grows. The intermediate multiplication in each step takes place on unary words whose lengths are already polynomial in the encoded formula and cap, even when the untruncated total product would be exponential.

`ExactProductBudgetSAT` chooses the cap one larger than the budget, so a capped result no larger than the budget is equivalent to the true product fitting. It intersects the measured budget guard, the reference well-formedness language, and the branch decider's language. The exact set has canonical encodings, the width-product bound, and satisfiability. The product of widths avoids the two-bit power-of-two overestimate used in the separate ternary-only construction, and it can also accommodate a few clauses of unbounded width. The P theorem is for the explicitly bounded language. There is no claimed reduction from unrestricted SAT to this family.
